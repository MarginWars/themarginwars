## 1. The short version

Avi is not building a "payments celebrity" brand. He is a payments operator with a long record of moving money at scale, and the brand exists to do one job: make it easy for the right buyers (acquirers, PSPs, banks and corporate treasurers facing stablecoin, treasury and money-movement decisions) to find him, trust him and hire him. Advisory, teardowns, briefings and Working Sessions are the product. Speaking and the invite-only Margin Wars Briefing are distribution and client development, not reach.

- **Positioning in one line (from 10):** "The operator's playbook for winning back payment margin, one tactic at a time, in every market you sell in."
- **Format:** one tactic per piece: mechanics, prerequisites, metrics and an impact range (sourced or labelled as an assumption), failure modes, owner, a cross-market column and a war-story slot (sanitised and approved by Avi). No thematic essays.
- **Tone:** understated, specific, practical. No hype, no predictions for applause, no "thought leader" language.

## 2. Niche thesis

1. Stablecoins are moving from crypto-native companies to incumbent payment rails. The incumbents (acquirers, PSPs, banks, large merchants and corporate treasurers) now have to decide whether to build, partner or buy.
2. The hard parts are not the token. They are the ledger, reconciliation, liquidity and treasury, settlement timing, compliance operations, partner diligence and the economics after fees. These are operator problems, and most of the public conversation skips them.
3. Buyers making board-level decisions want someone who has run money movement in production, can say what breaks at scale, and can turn that into a business case a board will approve.
4. That buyer is global. Cross-border flows, emerging-market rails and multi-currency treasury are where stablecoins are most likely to matter first, so a perspective that spans the US, UK/Europe and Africa is a real edge.
5. Few people combine current operating credibility at a top-tier payments company with independent, board-facing advisory work on this exact decision. That is the niche.

## 3. Positioning

## 6. Voice and tone rules

- Lead with the problem, not the opinion. Show the mechanism before the conclusion.
- Use numbers only when they are verified and publishable; otherwise describe the shape of the problem.
- Prefer "here is what broke and what we changed" over "here is the future".
- No engagement bait, no hot takes for reach, no emoji-led posts, no "thrilled to announce".
- Credit teams, not self. "We" for operating stories; "I" for opinions.
- Every piece ends with something a reader can use on Monday: a checklist, a question, a decision rule.

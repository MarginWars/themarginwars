## 0. Bottom line
**Recommendation: Option A, "The Margin Wars".** Avi becomes the operator who shows payments and finance leaders how to win back basis points, market by market, across four regions. It is delivered through an invite-only tactical briefing and closed-door "tactic working sessions", and it is anchored on one proprietary asset: the **Cross-Market Margin Playbook**, a tactics library that compares the same lever (routing, network tokens, retries, IC++, chargebacks, FX, PayFac economics, stablecoin rails) across the US, UK/EU, Africa and APAC.

> **Positioning line:** *"The operator's playbook for winning back payment margin, one tactic at a time, in every market you sell in."*

**What the landscape shows**
1. **Media voices are thematic.** Mikula, Rubinstein, Taylor, This Week in Fintech, Payments Dive, Payments NEXT and The Paypers sell news or theses, not playbooks.
2. **Tactical depth sits in firms**, namely CMSPI, Glenbrook and Flagship. That work is project-priced, delivered by teams, and not tied to a personal operator voice. CMSPI's moat is its data (benchmarks across "trillions in consumer spend") [11].
3. **Individual tactical consultants such as Stapp are US-centred** and generalist. Stapp also offers fractional commercial roles, which pulls her toward GTM and away from margin [17].
4. **Stablecoin commentary is about adoption and theses.** Almost nobody frames it as a line-item cost play to set against interchange, FX and correspondent fees. Artemis comes closest with B2B data [20].
5. **Optimisation content tends to bundle the levers generically** ("interchange, tokenization, routing, retry, chargeback recovery" [22]). It is not comparative across markets.

## 2. White space and unfair advantages
**White space [Assumption, derived from §1]:** a *named individual operator* who publishes *tactic-level*, *cross-market comparative*, *margin-denominated* guidance and then works the tactic live with a small peer group. Firms have the depth but no operator voice. Voices have reach but no tactics. Nobody compares the same lever across the US, UK/EU, Africa and APAC, and nobody puts stablecoin rails into the same cost ledger as cards.

## 3. Three positioning options
**A. The Margin Wars (recommended).** An operator-led tactics library plus invite-only briefing and working sessions, compared across markets.
**C. Red Team Your Payments Stack.** A pure diagnostic service, close to CMSPI/Glenbrook/Flagship. That puts Avi up against their benchmark data and teams, and a solo operator lacks the proprietary dataset [11].

**Why A wins:** it keeps B as one chapter (stablecoin rails as a lever) and C as a product (the teardown), but competes on the one thing firms and media cannot replicate: a single operator's cross-market judgement.

### Signature asset: the Cross-Market Margin Playbook
- Each tactic gets one card: **lever → mechanics → US / UK-EU / Africa / APAC differences → typical margin impact range (from public sources only) → failure modes → "what I'd do Monday"**.
- Launch with 12 cards: debit/least-cost routing, network tokens, retry/dunning logic, IC++ versus blended pricing, chargeback/representment, Visa CEDP and commercial-card data [25], FX markup and local acquiring, PayFac/sub-merchant economics, marketplace payouts, APMs/pay-by-bank, orchestration lock-in, stablecoin settlement as a cost line.

### Proof points needed (within 90 days)
1. Twelve published Playbook cards with sources, which act as the portfolio.
2. Three Working Sessions run, with named attendee titles (not companies) and NPS.
3. Two to three teardown case notes, anonymised, with client permission and a basis-point outcome.
4. One stage slot recut as "tactic" content, building on the Money20/20 and MRC credentials.

### Risks and mitigations
- **Capacity:** an executive role plus a six-month engagement limits delivery. Cap the work at about 2 days a month to start.
- **Commoditisation:** the generic optimisation lists already exist [22][23]. Differentiation has to come from cross-market comparison and from "war story" failure modes.
- **Benchmark trap:** don't claim a proprietary dataset he doesn't have. That is CMSPI's ground [11].

## 4. Kill criteria: when this becomes "just another payments voice"
1. Any issue that comments on news or trends without a specific, actionable lever.
2. Content that is US-only, or that has no cross-market column.
3. Impact numbers that aren't sourced, or that rely on employer data.
4. Chasing audience size: an open Substack, LinkedIn hot takes, or the subscriber count used as the KPI.
5. A stablecoin take that is a thesis, not a cost line.
6. Becoming a regular podcast guest instead of running his own room.
7. Fewer than 3 paid teardowns or retainers within 6 months of launch, or session attendees not returning (under 40% repeat) [Assumption: thresholds].

## 4. Why now

Each point is drawn from 01 (niche thesis) and 10 (competitive landscape). These are judgements, not market data.

1. **The media is thematic.** The main payments and fintech voices sell news or theses, not playbooks (10 §1).
2. **The tactical depth is locked inside firms.** It is priced by the project and delivered by teams, with no named operator voice (10 §1).
3. **Optimisation advice is generic and single-market.** Lists bundle "interchange, tokenization, routing, retry, chargeback recovery" without comparing the same lever across markets (10 §1).
4. **Stablecoins are reaching incumbent rails, and almost nobody treats them as a cost line.** Acquirers, PSPs, banks and large merchants now face build, partner or buy decisions. The public conversation is about adoption, not about setting stablecoin settlement against interchange, FX and correspondent fees (01 §2; 10 §1).
5. **Payment cost moves fast.** It is one of the few P&L lines an operator can move within a quarter without a new product (09, Talk 1).
6. **Agentic checkout is a new front, not the whole war.** Frontier model labs moving into the money path bring a reverse money flow and fresh merchant-of-record, tax and scheme questions, sitting alongside the core fronts that classic optimisation content does not cover [TBC: cite publicly announced agentic-checkout partnerships and dates] (`writing/play_llm_payments_DRAFT.md`).

**Buyers of advisory** (06 Advisory): leadership teams with a margin decision in front of them, such as routing, acquirer markup, disputes, FX placement and local acquiring, or stablecoin settlement against the current cross-border rail.

## 8. Voice rules

**Register.** Understated, specific, practical. War lingo gives the brand its structure; it never stands in for substance.

1. **Lead with the problem, then the mechanism, then the conclusion.** If in doubt, cut the adjective and add the mechanism (01 §3, §6).
2. **Tactics, not themes.** Every piece names one lever and ends with something a reader can use on Monday: a checklist, a question or a decision rule (01 §6; 10 kill criteria 1 and 5).
3. **Every piece has a cross-continent campaign column** for the US, UK/EU, Africa and APAC. A US-only piece fails the kill test (10, criterion 2).
4. **Numbers only when they are sourced or labelled as an assumption.** Never use employer or client data (10, criterion 3).
5. **War lingo, used with discipline.**
   - Use: *Margin War I / II / III*, *campaign*, *cross-continent campaign*, *front*, *the move*, *battlefield* (market or segment), *failure mode*, *Working Session*.
   - War words name structure (headings, series, sections). The sentence underneath stays plain operator English.
   - No violence, casualty or "kill the competition" imagery. No references to real conflicts. Never "war on" a company, a regulator or a scheme.
7. **"Payments OG" is internal shorthand.** Use it at most once per piece, in the one-liner or the banner tone, never as a self-description in a bio.
8. **No hype.** No "thought leader", no "thrilled to announce", no emoji-led posts, no polls, no "agree?", no follower or subscriber counts (01 §6; 06; 10, criterion 4).
9. **Naming and tense rules (hard rules).**
   - Anitha: never use "currently" or "today", and never refer to a change of role. Use her approved lines only.
   - First names only. The single exception is the legal footer, "© 2026 Avi Reddy Advisory".
   - Never mention or allude to any name on the restricted list (the anchor advisory client and its brands, and the other excluded companies on Avi's list; the list is deliberately not reproduced in collateral), or to any patent application or filing number.
10. **Disclaimer on every piece:** "Views are our own and do not represent any employer or client." Stories are shared with details removed or changed so that no employer, client or customer can be identified.
11. **Mark drafts.** Every draft carries **DRAFT FOR REVIEW** until both co-authors sign off.

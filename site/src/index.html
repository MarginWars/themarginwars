01

Where margin goes missing

Most payment cost doesn't show up as one big number. It shows up as a hundred small ones: an interchange downgrade on a card you never meant to accept that way, a scheme fee nobody negotiated, an FX markup buried in the settlement rate, a decline that cost a sale, a route that sends volume to the expensive acquirer, a cross-border fee on a payment that could have been local. Each one looks too small to chase. Together they're the margin.

02

What we do about it

We work with payments and finance leaders on specific moves: reporting net revenue instead of headline TPV, choosing merchant-of-record structures flow by flow, quoting FX markup corridor by corridor, moving a corridor to local rails, pricing PayFac, embedded and enterprise deals on who carries the loss, and mapping the licence, scheme and tax exposure that follows a big flow. For every move, we show how it plays in the US, UK/EU, Africa and APAC.

03

Fronts

01

Interchange

The problem. Transactions qualify for a worse rate than they should because of missing data, the wrong card-present or card-not-present flags, or late capture.

Our move. We sort downgrades by cause and fix the data or flow behind each one.

02

Scheme fees

The problem. Network fees arrive as dozens of line items in different formats from each acquirer, and nobody checks them.

Our move. We map every line to the rule behind it and flag the ones you shouldn't be paying.

03

FX

The problem. The markup sits inside the rate, so nobody quotes it or compares it across corridors.

Our move. We quote the markup per corridor and per order, then decide where it should sit.

04

Acceptance

The problem. Declines and authentication friction cost sales that never reach the cost report.

Our move. We put the cost of a decline next to the cost of a fee, so the two can be traded off openly.

05

Routing

The problem. Volume goes to whichever acquirer it went to last year, not to the one that prices it best today.

Our move. We set routing rules on all-in cost and approval, and keep a reserve of volume you can move.

06

Cross-border

The problem. Payments that could settle locally pay cross-border fees and FX twice.

Our move. We pick a corridor, test local rails or account-to-account payments, and measure the result.

How engagements run

  1. 01

    Scope. A short call to name the number you want to move. Then a written proposal with a fixed fee and one clear deliverable.

  2. 02

    Clear the ground. We run a conflict check before any confidential detail changes hands. If a matter conflicts with an existing commitment, we decline it.

  3. 03

    Work quietly. A confidentiality agreement is standard. We use client names only with written permission.

  4. 04

    Hand over. You get the list of moves, the owner for each one, and the metric that will show whether it worked.

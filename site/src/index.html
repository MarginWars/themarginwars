01

Where margin goes missing

Not one big number. A hundred small ones: downgrades, unchecked scheme fees, hidden FX, soft declines, lazy routing, avoidable cross-border fees. Together, they're the margin.

02

What we do about it

Specific moves: net revenue over TPV, merchant of record by flow, FX by corridor, local rails, loss-based PayFac pricing. Each shown for US, UK/EU, Africa and APAC.

03

Fronts

01

Interchange

The problem. Downgrades from missing Level 2/3 data, wrong card-present flags, no AVS, late capture.

Our move. Sort the qualification report by reason code. Fix the biggest bucket first.

02

Scheme fees

The problem. Dozens of network fee lines per acquirer. Nobody checks them.

Our move. Map each line to its rule. Flag what you shouldn't pay.

03

FX

The problem. The markup hides inside the rate.

Our move. Quote it per corridor, per order. Then decide where it sits.

04

Acceptance

The problem. Declines and 3DS friction lose sales the cost report never shows.

Our move. Soft vs hard declines, smart retries, network tokens, account updater, 3DS exemptions. Price each lost sale against the fee.

05

Routing

The problem. Volume goes where it went last year.

Our move. Route on all-in cost per approved order. LCR on debit, tested failover, portable volume for leverage.

06

Cross-border

The problem. Local-eligible payments pay cross-border fees and FX twice.

Our move. One corridor: local acquiring or A2A vs a control, on cost, approvals and settlement days. Stablecoins, same scorecard.

United States · Bank transfer

US bank transfer

Updated 8 Nov 2026 · Preview · Linked rule context ↗
Payment instructionYour business
Recipient accountDomestic US

Routes to investigate

  • ACHUS bank rail: pay-ins and payouts, run under Nacha rules
  • RTPUS real-time payments network operated by The Clearing House
  • FedNowUS instant payments, operated by the Federal Reserve

Alternatives, not legs. Each prices differently; your provider decides eligibility.

Who sets the rules

  • NachaGoverns the ACH Network rules
  • Federal ReserveCentral bank; operates FedNow
  • CFPBConsumer financial protection regulator
  • FinCENAnti-money-laundering authority under the Bank Secrecy Act

Three checks before you move

  1. 01
    Where the fee becomes a lossProcessing cost vs returns, exceptions and ops effort.
  2. 02
    Who can use the routeEligibility, limits, access. A listing isn't approval.
  3. 03
    Who owns the exceptionSettlement, recon and recovery path, before you switch.

What we do not know

Your bank or provider access, contract, price, limits and return exposure.

Routes to investigate

What we do not know

Your pricing model (blended or pass-through), downgrade volume, and how your acquirer routes debit today.

UK / EU · Cards

UK / EU card acceptance

Updated 8 Nov 2026 · Preview · Linked rule context ↗
Card paymentYour checkout
Issuer authentication3DS and SCA

Routes to investigate

  • 3DS exemptionsWhere approvals are won or lost under strong customer authentication
  • Bank debitEnabled market by market across the EU
  • Open bankingAccount-to-account payment, enabled market by market

Authentication decides conversion. Alternatives depend on the market and your provider.

Who sets the rules

  • EBAEU banking authority; PSD2 and SCA technical standards
  • FCAAuthorises payment and e-money institutions
  • PSRPayment Systems Regulator
  • EMVCoSets the EMV 3-D Secure specification
  • UK FinanceUK banking and finance trade association
  • European Payments CouncilManages the SEPA payment schemes

Three checks before you move

  1. 01
    Where friction costs the salePrice 3DS drop-off against the fee line.
  2. 02
    Which exemptions you useCheck which SCA exemptions your provider applies, and on which flows.
  3. 03
    Which market needs a local methodWhere bank debit or open banking beats a card.

Rule status, dated

3DS / SCA
EU: SCA under PSD2, with EBA technical standards. UK: SCA under FCA rules.
PSD3 / PSR (EU)
Not in force. Council adopted its first-reading position on 29 Oct 2026; Parliament's second reading is pending. PSD2 still applies.

What we do not know

Your exemption rates, issuer behaviour by market, and which local methods your provider supports.

Australia & NZ · Cards

Australia & NZ card acceptance

Updated 8 Nov 2026 · Preview · Linked rule context ↗
Card paymentYour checkout
AcquirerDual-network debit

ExperienceAnitha set up Braintree Australia, twice.

Routes to investigate

  • Least-cost routingSend dual-network debit via the network that costs less. The RBA expects acquirers to offer and promote it.
  • eftposAustralia's domestic debit card scheme
  • Bank acquirersANZ, CBA, NAB and Westpac report LCR take-up to the RBA

Options to investigate. LCR can be binary, threshold or dynamic, and your provider sets which.

Who sets the rules

  • RBAPayments System Board; regulates designated card networks
  • AusPayNetIndustry body; runs the CNP Fraud Mitigation Framework
  • Commerce CommissionNZ; regulates Mastercard and Visa interchange under the Retail Payment System Act 2022
  • Payments NZGoverns the rules for NZ's core payment systems

Three checks before you move

  1. 01
    Is LCR on, and which kindCheck binary, threshold or dynamic routing, in person and online.
  2. 02
    Where the surcharge used to sitPrice the acceptance cost you can no longer pass on in Australia.
  3. 03
    Did the cut reach your feeCompare your rate with the pass-through large acquirers must publish from 30 Jan 2027.

Rule status, dated

AU surcharging
Removed from 1 Oct 2026 on eftpos, Mastercard and Visa.
AU interchange
Lower domestic caps from 1 Oct 2026; a cap on foreign-issued cards from 1 Apr 2027. Online and wallet LCR is under further RBA consultation.
NZ interchange
Personal-card limits from 1 Dec 2025 (domestic) and 1 May 2026 (foreign); commercial credit limits from 22 Apr 2027.
3DS / SCA
AU: AusPayNet requires SCA once a merchant breaches its fraud threshold for consecutive quarters. NZ: not yet sourced.

What we do not know

Your plan type, LCR setting, pass-through, and whether NZ surcharging rules change.

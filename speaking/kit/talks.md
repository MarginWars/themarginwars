## Talk 1. Payment tactics for margin wars

*Keynote or workshop, 30-45 min. Best fit: Money20/20 Europe ("New Rails, New Rules", workshop), Money20/20 Asia, Fintech Meetup, PAY360.*

**Abstract (100 words).** Payment cost is one of the few P&L lines an operator can move inside a quarter without shipping a new product. This session works through six moves, one at a time: price on net revenue and take rate, not headline TPV; choose agent or merchant of record flow by flow; quote the FX markup per corridor, per order; move one corridor to local rails or account-to-account; price the agent channel; and map licence and tax exposure before a flow gets big. Each move comes with its mechanics, the owner, the proof metric and the rollback trigger, plus how it changes across the US, UK/EU, Africa and APAC.

**Takeaways**

1. A one-page test for whether your deals are priced on net margin or on volume.
2. How to find, per corridor, where the FX markup sits and who keeps it.
3. A move-by-move checklist: owner, proof metric and rollback trigger for each.

## Talk 2. PayFac and AI: tactics for sub-merchant margin

*30 min talk, panel anchor or fireside. Builds on the Fiserv Forum 2024 panel "Future is PayFac and GenAI". Best fit: Fintech Meetup, ETA TRANSACT (2028 call), Nacha.*

**Abstract (100 words).** Becoming a payment facilitator changes who keeps the margin and who carries the loss. This session works through the levers a platform actually controls: sub-merchant pricing (pass-through or blended), onboarding and underwriting cost, reserve and payout policy, and residual splits with the sponsor acquirer. It then separates where AI already earns its place, such as document checks, risk triage and support deflection, from where it adds risk. Every lever comes with the metric to track and the failure mode to watch, so platform, risk and finance teams leave with the same scorecard for embedded payments.

**Takeaways**

1. Price PayFac and embedded payments on who carries the loss, not on headline rate.
2. The four levers a platform controls, and the metric that proves each one.
3. Where AI cuts underwriting and support cost today, and where it adds risk.

## Talk 3. Marketplace payments and risk: payout, reserve and loss tactics

*30 min talk or hosted conversation. Builds on hosting "Marketplaces on the Rise: Payments and Risk" at MRC Las Vegas 2024. Best fit: MRC (SME form), Fintech Meetup, Money20/20 Asia.*

**Abstract (100 words).** Marketplaces lose margin in three places: fraud and chargebacks, money held or paid out at the wrong time, and the cost of checking sellers at scale. This session takes one tactic in each. First, payout timing and holds set by seller risk tier. Second, rolling reserves sized to real exposure rather than to habit. Third, a representment workflow that decides which chargebacks are worth fighting. For each tactic it covers the data you need, who owns it, how it fails when set too loose or too tight, and how the rules change by market, from the US and UK/EU to APAC.

**Takeaways**

1. A risk-tiered payout and hold schedule you can test on one seller cohort.
2. How to size a rolling reserve to exposure, and when to release it.
3. A rule for which chargebacks to fight, priced against the cost of fighting.

## Talk 4. Merchant data and cloud data sharing: finding basis points in your own data

*30 min talk or co-presented case session. Builds on Snowflake Summit 2025 (keynote co-presenter with Fiserv) and Money20/20 USA 2024 (with Snowflake). Best fit: Money20/20 USA 2027, Money20/20 Europe, Nacha.*

**Abstract (100 words).** Most merchants already hold the data to find lost margin, but it sits in their processor's systems, not their own. This session shows how direct processor-to-merchant data sharing, with no file exports and no copies, supports three tactics. First, approval-rate analysis by BIN and issuer to decide routing. Second, decline-code analysis that feeds a soft-decline retry schedule. Third, a line-by-line check of fees against an interchange++ statement. It draws on zero-copy work that cut a merchant data SLA from 96 hours to 12, and covers what to ask your processor for, who owns the analysis, and the governance traps.

**Takeaways**

1. The three data cuts that pay for themselves: approvals by BIN, decline codes and fee lines.
2. What to ask your processor for, and how to word the data-sharing request.
3. The governance traps (ownership, retention, access) to settle before the first query.

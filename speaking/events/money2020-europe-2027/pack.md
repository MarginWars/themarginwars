Session title: Six rails, one P&L: the margin moves that survive Europe's multirail shift
Description:
Payment cost is one of the few P&L lines an operator can move inside a quarter without shipping a new product. As cards, instant payments, open banking, wallets and stablecoins compete for the same checkout, the question is no longer which rail wins but who keeps the margin on each one. This working session takes six moves one at a time. Price on net revenue and take rate, not headline TPV. Choose agent or merchant of record flow by flow. Quote the FX markup per corridor, per order. Move one corridor to local rails or account-to-account. Price the agent channel. Map licence and tax exposure before a flow gets big. Each move comes with its mechanics, the owner, the proof metric and the rollback trigger, and how it changes between Europe, the US, Africa and APAC. Stablecoin settlement is treated only as one more cost line. No product pitch.
Takeaways:
1. A one-page test for whether your deals are priced on net margin or on volume.
2. How to find, per corridor, where the FX markup sits and who keeps it.
3. A move-by-move checklist: owner, proof metric and rollback trigger for each.
Why this matters now (if asked): Europe is rethinking who builds and governs its payment rails (the 2027 theme is "Control is Changing Hands"). Operators who add rails without re-pricing them hand margin to whoever controls the rail.
Proof point (anonymised; approved by Avi 21 Oct 2026):
In 2025 a team I lead at a large payments network delivered over $76M in incremental transaction margin: $40M+ from value-added-service adoption across existing merchants, $29M+ from pricing and go-to-market optimisation for new merchants, and $7M+ from systematic margin improvements. The workshop breaks those three buckets into individual moves, with the metric and failure mode for each.

Website: https://themarginwars.com | LinkedIn company page: https://www.linkedin.com/company/144081046/
Session title: Pay out later, lose less: three payout and reserve tactics for cross-border marketplaces
Format: 30-minute session (or hosted roundtable if that's the only matching option)
Track/topic: chargebacks / payments and risk – pick the closest option in the form
Audience: marketplace payments, risk and finance leads; merchants; PSPs
Abstract:
Marketplaces lose margin in three places: fraud and chargebacks, money paid out at the wrong time, and the cost of checking sellers. This session works through one tactic in each. (1) Set payout timing and holds by seller risk tier. (2) Size rolling reserves to real exposure, corridor by corridor, including Pix-speed payouts. (3) Run a representment workflow that decides which disputes to fight. For each tactic the session covers the data needed, who owns it, the metric that proves it worked, an impact range, and how it fails when set too loose or too tight. It also shows how each tactic changes between Brazil, the US and Europe. Attendees leave with one tactic card per lever. No product pitch.
Speaker bio (employer unnamed, per the draft):
Avi Reddy is a senior product director in enterprise payments, with P&L and go-to-market for a large acquiring platform. His team turned a loss-making acquiring platform profitable: a billion-dollar transaction-margin swing. He has worked across the US, UK/EU, Eurasia, South Africa, India and APAC, co-founded Rentoza and Effectus, and has spoken at Snowflake Summit 2025, Money20/20 USA 2024, Fiserv Forum 2024 and MRC Las Vegas 2024, where he hosted "Marketplaces on the Rise". He writes The Margin Wars: payment tactics from the front lines.
Relevant experience / why you (if asked):
A merchant account limitation error at a large payments platform cascaded across systems and put more than $96M of a major merchant's money at risk. I led the response with bank partners, product, engineering, operations and treasury, and we resolved it. Afterward we built incident playbooks: who decides, which funds move first, and how payouts and holds are handled while systems are wrong. This session's payout-hold and reserve tactics come from that experience: set them before the incident, because during one you are deciding with partial data.

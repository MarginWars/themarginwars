Your debit volume goes where it went last year. Nobody decided that. It just happened.

Under Regulation II, US debit cards carry two unaffiliated networks, and the merchant gets to pick the route. So who on your team is actually picking?

And the spread is real. Federal Reserve data for 2024: covered debit averaged $0.23 in interchange per transaction. Exempt debit averaged $0.51.

The Monday move:
1. Pull last month's debit volume by network.
2. Split it covered vs exempt. Know your issuer mix before you price anything.
3. Ask your acquirer one question, in writing: are you doing least-cost routing on our debit, or defaulting?
4. Judge the answer on all-in cost per approved order, not cost per transaction. A cheaper route that loses approvals isn't cheaper.

Routing is the one fee lever most merchants already own. Use it.

Question for the payments people who've actually switched it on: what moved first, cost per order, approval rate, or your acquirer's willingness to talk? And what would you check before you trusted the saving?
